# House Hunting

Every Singaporean here says, “Everythiing in Singapore is great, except for the weather”. However, for expats, there is one more catch: the notoriously high rent.

I used PropertyGuru for my search. Once you find a unit you like, you should contact the agent. On property, You can easily do this by clicking the red ‘Call' icon. PropertyGuru automatically shares your enqury details with agents, while giving you their direct contact info.

### Viewing

the Agents will kindly assist you. Agents will assess if your profile matches the landlord's requirements. If it's a good fit, or if you can consider a stretch, You can request viewing, and the agent will coordinate the schedules for you if possible.

**Important** : I highly recommend viewing  properties directly with the agent who posted the listing. In Singapore, the landlords covers the commission if you deal directly with the landlord's agent, who post the listing on PropertyGuru. However, If you ask an agent to help viewing another units in the same development just for convenience, you are essentially hiring them as your own agent! In that case, you will have to pay for the agent, as you hire him to coordinate those extra viewings.

### Offering

You can make offers on units you like after viewings. (Obviously you can skip the viewing, but I don't recommend it.) You could put in multiple offers at once. In my case, I made three. It's totally normal to go back and forth a time or two with the landlord regarding the rent. You can always ask for discount, but always keep in mind that ‘hot’ units are always snapped up at the full asking price or even higher.\
\
As a Korean, I emphasize that I will keep the unit extremely clean and free of smells.

Just a heads up: “partially furnished” usually means that, only appliances like a refrigerator, ovens and washing machine, are included.

### LOI letter & Deposit

If you and the LL successfully reach an agreement regarding the rent, you will receive an LOI(Letter of Intent) from the agent. Think of the LOI as a ‘pre-contract' that outlines the basic and most important terms of your agreement. Review the claims carefully; if everything looks good, you will have to make a deposit. A deposit is usually equal to 1 or 2 months' rent.

Singapore's law is brutal, so you don't need to worry deeply about rental fraud. However, For peace of mind, you can easily verify the agent's credential and the landlord's legitimacy.

* Agent Credential : [https://www.cea.gov.sg/](https://www.cea.gov.sg/)
* Landlord legitimacy : Ask your agent an IRSA (property tax) document to prove the ownership

### TA

If you sign the LOI and make the deposit, you will receive the TA (Tenancy Agreement). This is an official contract, so you should read it very very carefully. If everything looks good, go ahead and sign it. Of course, you should ask your agent if you have any questions or you want to request ammendments. And just like that, you're done! The Agent will handover the unit to you on the move-in date. \
